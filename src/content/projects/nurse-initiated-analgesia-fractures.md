Time-to-analgesia for suspected long-bone fractures is a well-known pain point in emergency
care. This project prospectively tracks patients managed under a nurse-initiated analgesia
protocol introduced earlier this year, measuring time-to-first-analgesia and patient-reported
pain scores against historical, physician-initiated care.

You'll be involved in prospective data collection on the floor, including approaching patients
and taking informed consent for the study component — genuinely useful experience for anyone
considering a research career in emergency medicine — plus basic biostatistics to compare the
two care pathways once enough patients are enrolled.

Expected output is a conference abstract for an emergency medicine meeting, with the department
keen to use the results to support wider rollout of the protocol if it performs well. This is
an active, ongoing project with a supervisor available for regular in-person debriefs after
shifts.
